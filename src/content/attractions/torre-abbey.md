Standing proudly near Torquay’s seafront within 18 acres of sweeping parkland, **[Torre Abbey](https://www.torre-abbey.org.uk/home)** is Torbay’s most historic building and the best-preserved medieval monastery in Devon and Cornwall. Blending over 800 years of living history with an acclaimed art gallery and award-winning gardens, it is one of the defining landmarks of the English Riviera.

---

## A Short History & Its Significance in Torquay

Torre Abbey’s story is inseparable from the growth of Torquay itself (read the full timeline on the **[Torre Abbey About page](https://www.torre-abbey.org.uk/about)**):

- **1196 – The Abbey's Founding:** Founded on 25 March 1196 by William Brewer for six Premonstratensian ("White") canons, Torre Abbey grew over the centuries to become the wealthiest Premonstratensian monastery in England. Its monastic estates, agriculture, and trade laid the early foundations for Torquay's local economy and even its first harbour.
- **1539 & 1588 – Dissolution and the Spanish Barn:** Following Henry VIII’s Dissolution of the Monasteries in 1539, the abbey passed into private hands. In 1588, its medieval tithe barn gained lasting fame as **the Spanish Barn** when Sir Francis Drake imprisoned 397 sailors captured from the Spanish Armada flagship *Nuestra Señora del Rosario* inside its stone walls.
- **1662–1930 – The Cary Family Era:** The Cary family acquired the estate in 1662 and shaped it for nearly 300 years, weaving a grand Georgian mansion around the medieval monastic ruins and playing a central role in Torquay's social and civic life.
- **1930–Present – Torquay’s Cultural Hub:** Purchased by the local council in 1930, Torre Abbey was preserved for the public and transformed into an accredited museum, municipal art gallery, and vibrant venue for exhibitions and community events.

## What to Discover Inside & Out

- **Historic Rooms & Medieval Ruins:** Wander through atmospheric medieval undercrofts, monastic remains, and grand Georgian interiors that reveal eight centuries of changing architecture and daily life.
- **The Art Gallery:** Explore a permanent collection of over 600 works spanning the 18th century to the present day—including Pre-Raphaelite highlights—alongside major touring exhibitions.
- **The Spanish Barn:** Step inside the imposing 700-year-old limestone barn that once stored monastic tithes and later held Armada prisoners.
- **18 Acres of Gardens & Agatha Christie's Potent Plants:** Stroll through walled gardens, the Palm House, and the famous **Potent Plants Garden**, which cultivates the poisonous botanicals featured in the novels of Torquay-born **[Agatha Christie](/attractions/agatha-christie)**.
